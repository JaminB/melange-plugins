# Kindjal

A content plugin for Worms Ultimate Mayhem that shifts every weapon a dimension darker. A kindjal is a heavy fighting
dagger; in Dune it is the blade kept for close, personal violence. Melee weapons maim and launch, explosives hit harder
and throw worms much further, every weapon has a grimmer name, and three new weapons join the panel: the **Acid
Spitter**, the **Acid Flask** and the **Crucible**. Acid is real poison gas, and on the screen it clings to a worm's
skin, bubbles and smokes. New impact, launch, explosion and acid sounds play on top of the game's own. Every renamed
weapon and the three new ones carry their own 3D model (held, thrown, fired or flying; 37 mesh banks in all), and the
Airstrike and Super Airstrike helicopters are replaced. The icons, sounds and sprites are original work generated by
the scripts in `tools/`. The models are re-authored, but 18 of the 37 banks are clones of the game's own skinned
meshes and keep its rig, skin weights, animation clips and UV layout; see "What is derived from the game" below.

It needs Melange 0.9.0 or later: the sounds use `wum.audio`, the weapon names use `weaponText`, the panel and HUD
icons use `weaponIcons`, the models use `meshes`, and the Banana Bomb's seven bomblets need the `u32` weapon fields.

## It is a content mod

Kindjal changes the simulation, so **every player in an online match needs the same version of it**. Melange handles
that: a host with Kindjal refuses to start against a player who lacks it or has another version, the lobby says who and
what differs, and a match recorded with Kindjal replays only with Kindjal. Offline it just works. Everything the plugin
changes in the simulation happens once, at match start, identically on every machine, and is undone when the match
ends; the visuals and sounds are local and never feed back into the game.

The AI uses the re-statted weapons exactly as it used the originals (they are the same weapons), and treats the three
new ones as their base weapons.

### The new weapons

They sit in the three free panel cells (29, 39 and 40) and follow their base weapon's ammo and delay in the game style.

- **Acid Spitter** (Bazooka base): a direct-fire corrosive round, 35 damage, modest knockback; the burst is the gas
  canister's poison cloud, and the client paints the acid on every worm in reach.
- **Acid Flask** (Grenade base): shatters where it lands, no fuse, 25 damage, and two more acid bursts 40 units to
  each side, so the spill spreads.
- **Crucible** (Holy Hand Grenade base): 120 damage, radius 243, crater 168, knockback 1.35 over 195; no pause and
  no choir, it detonates when it settles.

Melange's weapon clones share their base's code; the ammo, delay, crate and AI behaviour are the base's.

## What you see and hear

All of this is client-side and off by a setting; none of it touches the simulation.

- **Acid on skin.** A worm caught in an acid burst gets a glossy green-yellow coat that spreads from spots to a full
  coat over about ten seconds, bubbles, and fades over about a minute; wisps of smoke rise from it. The coat is a
  screen-space pass on skin-coloured pixels inside the worm's body, after Bloodsand's blood if that plugin is on, so
  blood still shows through acid.
- **Acid bursts.** Green-yellow smoke and a bubbling puddle at the burst that smokes and dies over about twenty seconds
  (at most three puddles).
- **Melee impact.** A spray of dark sparks at the victim and a short red hit-flash on a bat, prod, punch or nail hit,
  with its own impact sound. Spike glints on the swing are an experimental setting, off by default.
- **Sounds.** A swing, four impacts, a launch thump, three explosion sizes, the Crucible and two acid sounds, all
  synthesised by `tools/make_sounds.py`, played through Melange's `wum.audio` on top of the game's own sounds with
  distance attenuation from the camera.
- **Icons.** Every renamed weapon has its own panel icon and HUD icon in the game's cel style, drawn by
  `tools/make_icons.py` and its group modules (`tools/icons_*.py`): a nail bat, a flaming fist, a shiv, a railspike,
  a black rocket, a pipe bomb, a powder keg, a profane orb, black bananas, a nail-studded cluster, a bear trap, a
  harpoon, a bomber, a falling carcass, a cracked stone donkey, a rabid sheep, a ram in a gas mask, a hooded crone with
  a noose, a hooded knifeman, a falling glutton, a dead star, a sawn-off, an elephant gun, a gallows turret, a
  dripping arrow and a rusted drum.
- **Models.** Every renamed weapon and the three new ones have their own held and/or projectile model (the Plantain
  Hell's bananas, the Nail Cluster and its pieces, the Plague Ram, the Hangwoman, the Knifeman, the Gorger and so on),
  and the Airstrike and Super Airstrike helicopters become a black and a bone-white gunship (`vehicleMeshes`). 37
  banks in all: 19 static meshes built by `tools/make_meshes.py` and `tools/build_meshes.py`, and 18 clones of vanilla
  skinned meshes built by `tools/clones_*.py` (see `tools/README-meshes.md`). Seen in play so far: the Nail Bat, Acid
  Flask and Crucible in hand; the rest are only checked by rebuilding them byte for byte, and have not been looked at in a match.

| Spike glints | off | Experimental billboards near the hand during a swing |

In a match the overlay's Lua menu has *Preview acid*, *Preview melee* and *Acid +10 s* for a look at the effects.

## With Bloodsand and Sunstone

Kindjal needs neither, and all three can run together. Kindjal's acid pass runs after Bloodsand's skin, guts and gibs
(order 54, against 51 to 53) and before Sunstone's occlusion and grade (100 and 300), and the hit-flash is the last
thing drawn of Kindjal's (Final 900, after Sunstone's lens at 800 and Bloodsand's lens splatter at 60). The client finds
the other two by their effect ids in `wum.postfx.list()` (checked at the start, at a match edge, when the mod list
changes and every five seconds) and adapts. It only ever switches or sets effects named `kindjal/...`.

- **Acid on Bloodsand's worms.** The acid's skin test is Bloodsand's (the same ratios of green and blue to red, hue and
  saturation) with one more limit: a hue under about 6 degrees is not skin, so Bloodsand's red blood, the raw pink of a
  wound and torn skin are left as they are and green-yellow acid does not paint over them. Bloodsand's green blood palette has
  green well above red and is cut by the skin test's upper limit. A worm Bloodsand is gibbing (health gone, though the
  game still calls it alive) loses its coat and wisps at once instead of steaming from nothing. The hit-flash is lowered to
  0.6 of its peak, because Bloodsand adds its own red splat on the lens for a hard hit near the camera.
- **Acid under Sunstone.** Sunstone's model lighting tints the sun side warm and the shade cold, which pushes skin in
  shade to more blue over red than the game does. When any Sunstone effect is on, the acid's skin test widens: a higher
  blue limit in the dark, a hue limit of 33 degrees instead of 30, a little more saturation, a lower brightness floor so skin in caves is still
  found (every limit is a ratio or relative to the pixel's own brightness, so Sunstone's exposure does not matter), and
  the acid is 15 percent more opaque (at the subtle intensity; at full it is already opaque). Both ease in over 0.3 s
  when Sunstone is switched on or off, rather than stepping, since the check can notice the change up to five seconds late. Sunstone's tonemap
  keeps a colour's hue and saturation and only moves brightness, so the acid's yellow-green (hue 64 to 80 degrees,
  saturation 0.76 to 0.80) comes out at the same hue and saturation, a little brighter in the darks and whiter only on
  the specular rim. Its colour grade at the default strength shifts it by about 2 degrees, and 4 in the darkest spots.
- **Cost.** The acid pass is on only while a coated worm is within half a screen of the view. It rejects pixels off every
  coated worm's body with a sphere test, then off its skin shell, and runs the colour test before the surface normal and
  the long painting code. Estimated (counted from the operations, not measured) at about 0.1 ms at 1080p. At Sunstone's
  Ultra 2x2 supersampling, which has four times the pixels, the estimate is about 0.3 to 0.4 ms, which is over the 0.3 ms
  the pass was meant to stay under; treat that setting as unproven and read `gpuMs` in the Mirage/Post-FX panel. The cost
  is assumed to be mostly the full-screen fetch, which is also a guess.

What has been checked, and how: the effect orders, Sunstone's tints and tonemap (a Python port of its `display.glsl`
and `Saturation`, with its `golden.png` and `dusk.png` colour grades decoded) and Bloodsand's skin test were read from
the source, the new skin test was ported to Python and swept over 300 000 random colours (it admits no more than a wider
blue, hue and brightness window, and loses only reds below 13 degrees), and the client's detection, the opacity and
flash scaling, the gibbed-worm rule, the off-screen switch and the `kindjal/` check ran against a stub of the Lua API.
No standalone GLSL compiler was used on the shaders; the game's driver compiled both effects when Kindjal, Bloodsand
and Sunstone ran together (see Limits). Not checked: the real colours of skin under Sunstone are worked out from its
constants and not measured on frames, the GPU times above are estimates, and whether the lowered flash still reads
well next to Bloodsand's splat is a judgement for a person to make in play.

## Limits

- Every peer needs the plugin; see above. A peer with a different version is refused by the host.
- The acid coat has sixteen worm slots. Hats, glasses and clothes are not coated (the pass only paints skin).
- The melee victim is a guess (the worm whose health dropped within reach right after a swing), so a hit that
  pushes a worm out of reach before the damage lands may not get its flourish.
- The sounds are synthesised; they have been checked for format and level, not auditioned by the author before the
  first release.
- Verified in the game before the first release: the renames (panel and tag), every stat field applied, the three
  clones in the panel with their icons, the 26 replaced panel and HUD icons, all three plugins (Kindjal, Bloodsand,
  Sunstone) running together with every effect compiled and no Lua faults, the acid coat under Sunstone's grading, the acid coat, smoke, puddle and melee flourish, sounds playing, the Nail
  Bat, Acid Flask and Crucible models in hand, and a two-player LocalNet match with identical Wormsign hashes on both
  machines over six turns. Not yet seen in play: the three projectiles in flight, every other model (the other 34
  banks and the two gunships), and whether the Rust Canister's blast and the Gibbet Turret's shot numbers take effect.
- The Crucible follows the Holy Hand Grenade's ammo and delay: in the Standard style it reads "Delayed" until the
  third turn, like the grenade it is built on.

## What is derived from the game

Kindjal ships no copy of a game file, but some of the `.xom` banks in `mod/assets/meshes/` are built from vanilla data
read from `Bundl09.xom` at build time:

- **19 static banks** (`make_meshes.py`, `build_meshes.py`): geometry and textures are generated from scratch. Each bank
  borrows the material and shader setup of one vanilla mesh so that it is lit like the game's own models.
- **18 clone banks** (`clones_beasts.py`, `clones_people.py`, `clones_air.py`, `clones_guns.py`, `clones_props.py`,
  made with `xomtool clone`): the vanilla skinned mesh is kept as the base, so its skeleton, node names, skin weights,
  animation clips, texture stages, vertex count and order, and UV layout are the game's. Only the vertex positions
  (deformed) and the texture pixels (repainted per UV island) are new; the gunships also use the vanilla atlas as
  shading detail under the new colours. The Rabid Flock, Plague Ram, Carrion Drop's carcass, the Hangwoman, the
  Knifeman, the Gorger, both gunships, the Slug Gun, Gibbet Turret, Harpoon launcher and Harpoon, the Plague Arrow's
  bow, the Nail Cluster and its piece, the Carpet Bombing shell, the Bear Trap and the Dead Star are of this kind.

Rebuilding any of them needs a game install and `xomtool` (see `tools/README-meshes.md`).
